Accessibility

Treadwise is used at the tire, often with gloves on, in sun or at night. We build it to work with the accessibility features in iOS, and we want to hear where it falls short.

Features the app supports

These are the features we design and test for on iPhone and iPad. The App Store page lists the ones confirmed for the current version.

  • VoiceOver. Buttons, tire positions and readings have spoken labels. A reading is read as, for example, "6 thirty-seconds, plus or minus 1, estimate". Each tire on a map reads its position, its value and its wear band. Charts have a readable list beside them.
  • Larger text. Text follows your Text Size setting up to the largest accessibility sizes. Layouts stack instead of cutting off readings, prices or buttons.
  • Dark mode. Every screen has a full dark version.
  • Without color alone. Wear bands always show an icon and a word (Good, Watch, Replace soon, No reading in 30+ days), and tire maps add a pattern, so color is never the only signal.
  • Contrast. Text and controls meet contrast targets in light and dark mode, and the app follows Increase Contrast.
  • Reduce Motion. Movement turns into short fades, nothing spins, and numbers appear without counting up.
  • Large touch targets. Every control is at least 44 points, and the main buttons are larger for gloved hands.

Voice-guided scanning

On iPhones with Face ID, the depth scan uses the front camera, so the screen faces the tire and you can't see it. The app guides you with speech ("closer", "hold still"), vibration patterns and a chime when the scan is done. Spoken cues play at your device's volume, and each one also shows on screen.

If you are deaf or hard of hearing, the vibration patterns match every spoken cue. The rear LiDAR scan on Pro iPhones, the guided coin and wear-bar check and gauge entry are fully visual.

On iPad, the app offers the guided check and gauge entry. Depth scans are tuned for iPhone.

Signing inspection records

Inspection records ask for a signature. You can draw it with a finger, and VoiceOver users can type their name instead; a typed signature is marked as typed on the report.

Not supported yet

  • Voice Control: most of the app can be used by voice, but drawing a signature needs touch, so we don't list Voice Control as supported yet.
  • The app plays no video, so it has no captions or audio descriptions to offer.

Tell us what doesn't work

If something in the app is hard to use with your setup, write to qidientlabs@gmail.com or use Settings › Contact support in the app. Tell us your device, the accessibility features you use and the screen where it happened. A person reads every message.
