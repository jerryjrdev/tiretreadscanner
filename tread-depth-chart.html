Trucks, tractors and buses

FMCSA 393.75 guideline4/32 on steer tires, 2/32 on all other positions

Federal rule 49 CFR 393.75 sets the minimum for commercial vehicles. Tires on the front wheels of a bus, truck or truck tractor need at least 4/32 inch of tread at any point on a major tread groove. All other tires need at least 2/32 inch in a major groove. Measurements are not taken where tie bars, humps or fillets are.1

Cars and pickups

Common legal minimum 2/32Check your state's law

For passenger vehicles, 2/32 inch is the usual replacement line: AAA notes that current industry guidelines and many state laws set it there,2 and NHTSA says tires are not safe and should be replaced at 2/32.3 Laws differ by state.

Why many drivers replace sooner

AAA suggests shopping for new tires at 4/32.2 In AAA's test, tires worn to 4/32 needed about 87 feet more to stop on a wet road from 60 mph, and when new tires had stopped, the worn ones were still doing almost 40 mph.4

32nds to inches and millimeters

One 32nd of an inch is exactly 0.79375 mm, because an inch is 25.4 mm. Millimeters below are rounded to one decimal.

How to measure tread depth

  • Measure in a main groove, not on a wear bar or tie bar.
  • Check the outer, center and inner grooves. The lowest reading is the one that counts, and a difference between them can mean uneven wear.
  • On steer tires, check more than one spot around the tire.
  • A tread depth gauge gives you the number. The penny and quarter tests tell you which side of 2/32 or 4/32 you are on.
  • NHTSA suggests checking tread at least once a month.3
