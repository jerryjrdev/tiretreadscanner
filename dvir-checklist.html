What 49 CFR 396.11 covers

The rule asks drivers to prepare a written report at the end of each day's work on each vehicle they drove, covering at least the items on this checklist, and to sign it. Before the vehicle runs again, the carrier signs off that any listed defect was repaired or that a repair was not needed, and keeps the report, the repair sign-off and the driver review for three months. Electronic records are allowed.1

Two exceptions matter to small operators. A report is not required when no defect is found or reported, and the section does not apply to a carrier that operates only one commercial motor vehicle.1 Many owner-operators keep a daily record anyway, as a habit and as a record of what was checked and fixed.

Before driving, 49 CFR 396.13 asks the driver to be satisfied that the vehicle is in safe operating condition, to review the last report where one is required, and to sign it to acknowledge the repair sign-off.2

Why the tire line deserves more than a tick

  • At the 2026 Roadcheck, tires were the second most common vehicle violation inspectors found, about 1 in 5.3
  • Tires are the most common roadside repair for trucks.4
  • A roadside repair can cost about 4 times a shop repair.5

FMCSA 393.75 guideline4/32 on steer tires, 2/32 on all other positions, measured in a major tread groove

Writing the lowest groove for every position, rather than "tires OK", gives you a trend, and shows which tire is closest to the line. See the tread depth chart for 32nds in millimeters.6

This checklist is a record-keeping aid, not legal advice. Rules change; check the current text of 49 CFR 396.11 and 396.13 for your operation.
