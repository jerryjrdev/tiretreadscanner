Uneven tire wear

When one part of the tread wears faster than the rest, the tire is pointing at something: pressure, alignment or rotation. Here is how to spot each pattern, the possible cause, and what to do. Possible cause, have it checked.

How to spot it

Measure the outer, center and inner grooves of the same tire. If one part is clearly lower than the others, the wear is uneven. Treadwise flags it when grooves differ by 2/32 or more.

You can see some patterns by eye, but a gauge or a scan of every groove catches them earlier, while the rest of the tire still has plenty of tread.

The common patterns

Both shoulders low

The outer and inner edges are worn more than the center.

Possible cause: low inflation. Have it checked.

Center low

The middle of the tread is worn more than the edges.

Possible cause: over-inflation. Have it checked.

One side low

The inner or outer edge is worn more than the rest.

Possible cause: alignment. Have it checked.

Patchy, cupped or diagonal wear is harder to read. Rotation, balance and alignment all play a part, so have it checked by a tire shop.1

Why it is worth catching

  • NHTSA suggests rotating tires every 5,000 to 8,000 miles if your vehicle's manufacturer recommends it, or sooner if uneven wear appears. Rotation, balance and alignment can help tires last longer.1
  • Only about 1 in 5 drivers keep their tires properly inflated.1 Correct pressure can add about 4,700 miles to a tire's life.1
  • Every 1 psi low across your tires costs about 0.2% in fuel economy.2
  • For trucks, tires cost the average truck 5 cents a mile in 2025, up 6.4%.3

Checking pressure

The recommended pressure is on the placard in the driver's door jamb or in the owner's manual. In a 2025 survey, 45% of drivers didn't know where to find it.4

What a wear note is, and isn'tIt points to a possible cause. Only a tire or alignment check can confirm it.
