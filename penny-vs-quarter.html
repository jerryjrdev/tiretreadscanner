Penny test vs quarter test

Two coins, two lines. A penny shows whether your tread is down to about 2/32, the common legal minimum. A quarter shows whether you still have 4/32, where AAA suggests shopping for new tires.

More than half of drivers say they don't know how to check tread wear.1 If that's you, these two coins are the quickest place to start.

How to do each test

What each coin tells you
CoinHowWhat it means
PennyPut it in a groove with Lincoln's head upside down.If you can see the top of his head, the tread is at about 2/32 or less. NHTSA says to replace the tire.2
QuarterPut it in a groove with Washington's head down.If the top of his head stays hidden, you have at least 4/32.3

Reading the result

Coin results for a car or pickup
You seeDepthBand in the app
Quarter: head hiddenAt least 4/32Good
Quarter: head visible. Penny: head hiddenBetween about 2/32 and 4/32Watch
Penny: head visibleAbout 2/32 or lessReplace soon

Replace soon means below the guideline: verify with a gauge. AAA suggests shopping for new tires at 4/32 rather than waiting for 2/32.4 In its test, tires worn to 4/32 needed about 87 feet more to stop on a wet road from 60 mph.5

On trucks and buses

FMCSA 393.75 guideline4/32 on steer tires, 2/32 on all other positions

The two coin lines happen to match the federal minimums for commercial vehicles: the quarter line for steer tires, the penny line for drive and trailer tires.6 Near those lines a coin is too rough to rely on. Use a tread depth gauge and measure in a main groove, not on a wear bar or tie bar.

Wear bars: the check built into the tire

Tires have raised wear bars across the bottom of the grooves. NHTSA says that when the tread is even with them, it's time to replace the tire.2 About 1 in 6 drivers don't know their tires have them.7

Where coin tests fall short

  • They tell you which side of a line you are on, not a number.
  • Check the outer, center and inner grooves, and a few spots around the tire. A tire that is low on one side can look fine in the middle; see uneven tire wear.
  • Worn coins and stones in the groove throw the result off.
  • Nothing is recorded, so you can't see how fast a tire is wearing. The tread depth chart shows what each 32nd means in millimeters.
